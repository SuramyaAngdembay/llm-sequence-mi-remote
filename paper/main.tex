\begin{abstract}
We audit language models adapted to non-insider employee logs by separating
score construction, evaluation population, input sensitivity, and internal
intervention effects. A calendar-label audit corrects the observed CERT r4.2
and r6.2 populations to \rv{r42.my_positive_days} positive days from \rv{r42.my_positive_users} insiders and \rv{r62.my_positive_days} days from
\rv{r62.my_positive_users} insiders. The main adapters' training membership is unaffected, but several
label-dependent dictionaries, donors, and comparator fits require repair.
Corrected main-adapter fold day ROC is \rv{r42.unseen_users.day_roc} and \rv{r62.unseen_users.day_roc} against unseen users,
compared with \rv{r42.all_users.day_roc} and \rv{r62.all_users.day_roc} against general-population negatives. In a complete
r4.2 score decomposition, averaging behavior targets improves fold day ROC from
\rv{c32.full.day_roc} to \rv{c32.behavior_only.day_roc} and AP from \rv{c32.full.day_ap} to \rv{c32.behavior_only.day_ap} while retaining profile context.
This is score mitigation, not evidence of internal profile independence.
A corrected sparse-feature pilot completes scoring but fails one realized-control
check; its diagnostics do not establish attack specificity. We retain the
mechanistic method, report the verified results with their scope, and identify
the remaining dependencies before reinstating historical causal claims.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
High ranking quality does not identify which information an anomaly detector
uses. In employee activity logs, unusual behavior, profile novelty, and training
exposure can all influence a score. We ask how these influences can be separated
using frozen-model score decompositions, input manipulations, and internal
interventions. This recovery edition distinguishes established measurements
from claims still awaiting corrected experiments. Its contribution is an
explicit audit and evidence record, not a detector leaderboard or a new
mathematical theorem.
