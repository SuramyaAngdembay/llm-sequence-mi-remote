## Priority 1: one corrected mechanistic result (C13/C14/C17/C24/C26/C35)

**Question:** do the corrected selected directions change behavior-target loss more than physically matched controls, and is that contrast larger on incident-window than window-negative receivers?

**Reuse:** verified r4.2 adapter, corrected metadata and population, corrected layer-26 dictionary and ranking, fresh batch-one references, original failed-run artifacts. Use the existing dictionary hash and feature IDs from the active handoff rather than transcribing historical IDs.

**Changed inputs:** the explicitly separate `dictionary-control-bf16-rejection-v1` sampling policy. This is a new conditional control distribution, not a repair of the old protocol's status. Before choosing a full replay or exact reuse of unaffected responses, freeze the policy's dependencies for *every* affected condition; do not splice a successful single-token probe into the old result and call the old gate passed.

**Endpoints:** behavior-only selected-minus-isotropic and selected-minus-active contrasts on attacks; their paired attack-minus-window-negative specificity; same endpoints and declared user-bootstrap aggregation as the previous corrected pilot. Also save profile/full loss views for interpretation. No response-based feature, donor, threshold, or control selection.

**Gates:** exact adapter/tokenizer/dictionary/text hashes; corrected receiver and non-insider donor eligibility; disjoint declared discovery/ranking group; live baseline bridge; zero-edit/alpha-zero equality; exact token support; per-token realized norm tolerance 5%; complete paired endpoints; loss-class partition and finite checks. Failed original gates stay recorded. Norm matching alone does not imply semantic control matching.

**Outputs:** frozen manifest, each condition's loss sums/counts, requested and realized edits, all control attempts/rejections, per-user effects, matching failures, untouched original run and separate new-policy analysis.

**Measured reference:** previous full pilot, 158 pairs × 2 receivers × 15 conditions = 4,740 rows, took **2,752.02 seconds (45.9 min)** on an RTX 3070. The one-receiver new-policy probe took **33.42 seconds**, including loading and six conditions. Neither is an estimate for every historical causal run. Reserve a 90-minute ceiling only for an equivalently sized replay after a short new-policy timing check; stop at gates, not at a desired effect. The point of another run is valid measurement, not obtaining significance. Existing diagnostics do not suggest established attack specificity.

## Priority 2: corrected attribution and replication (C11–C24/C29)

**Question:** does the profile-versus-behavior activation-location pattern remain under corrected dictionary fitting, feature selection, and populations?

**Reuse:** full deltas where key coverage and numerical distribution are documented; adapter weights; raw tokens. Re-encode with the corrected dictionary and recompute ranking/attribution before scheduling interventions. Report attribution location separately from semantic dependence.

**Changed inputs:** all label-selected dictionary samples, rankings and controls; official-insider exclusion for non-insider references/donors. For C22, redraw the old label-selected negative pools as well as adding the previously missing 211 r4.2 / 11 r6.2 positive examples per seed. Benign-only dictionaries must exclude official insiders by user, not just positive days.

**Gates:** exact coverage ledger; reproducible token sampling and torch seeds; dictionary normalization/hash; discovery/confirmation policy; activation mass and token-class checks; declared numerical distribution. Predeclare one dictionary/configuration per intended contrast rather than searching a grid and reporting its best outcome.

**Outputs:** new encoding/ranking/attribution bundles and a dependency diff against historical runs; then only the intervention families retained in the paper. A changed dictionary prevents exact reuse of old intervention tuples.

**Timing:** not yet measured for this corrected full scope. First time a fixed-size encoding/ranking slice; report seconds/token, peak RAM and expected total token count. Do not extrapolate the old ~200 GPU-SU spend or the pilot's 45.9 minutes to this program.

## Priority 3: input swaps (C10) and profile-dependence sampling (C36/C39)

**Question:** how does profile substitution affect behavior prediction under corrected receiver classes and eligible partners?

**Reuse:** C10's 723 r4.2 and 60 r6.2 exact eligible fixed pairs only if the frozen target policy retains them. All fixed-input Pilot 2/H6 responses remain a separate exploratory record.

**Changed inputs:** add C10's 230/13 missing positive receivers; replace ineligible donor assignments for existing corrected-positive r4.2 receivers; reconstruct C36/C39 class matching rather than merely renaming labels. Require partner users absent from the official insider list. Freeze same-department/other-department/training-partner policies and deterministic matching before inference.

**Endpoints:** swap-minus-original behavior-target loss, adapted and adapter-disabled base on identical inputs; adapted-minus-base; corrected within-user class contrast. Full/profile loss views are secondary. An input-swap experiment does not identify SAE mediation.

**Gates:** partner membership, same-user exclusion, actual text-only profile change, current-day target boundary, model/tokenizer and same-batch reference equality. Reuse is invalid if the changed batching changes the paired reference. Save exact edited input text hashes and partner identities.

**Outputs:** frozen input/partner manifest; all original/swapped per-example target losses and counts; eligibility exclusions; coverage of every corrected positive; fixed-cohort versus newly sampled estimates separated.

**Timing:** measure 32 receivers through the complete declared set of variants at batch size one, including original references. Derive the total only after constructing the final eligible input manifest. No adapter retraining is needed for these frozen-model tests.

## Priority 4: comparison models (C27/C45/C46)

**Question:** how do comparison architectures perform under explicitly matched, corrected training exposure and evaluation populations? No current corrected architecture superiority claim is made.

**Reuse:** raw session-feature tables and source code; C46's 26 r4.2 clean-training bundles only for a protocol whose unchanged scaler/training membership can be proved. All other affected fitting requires refit. C45 lacks saved per-example scores/checkpoints; old fold summary rows cannot be relabeled.

**Changed inputs:** form non-insider users from the official list, not a post-join positive-user set; corrected day targets; train/validation/test membership and scaler fitting. A fair LM comparison must use the main adapter's actual training exposure or clearly describe a distinct protocol. Do not mix old per-fold baseline exposure with fixed-LM exposure and call them equal.

**Frozen endpoints:** day ROC, AP and declared operating-point recall; user-max ROC/AP; per-insider fold values, with equal-insider versus pooled results kept separate. Mechanistic comparisons require reranking and intervention reruns after model refitting.

**Gates:** no official insiders or test users in fit/scaler pools; complete corrected population coverage; deterministic seeds; train-key and feature-schema hashes; checkpoint reload/score equality; one shared evaluator and score direction.

**Outputs:** folds, actual scaler/train keys, scaler, model checkpoint, preprocessing config, per-example predictions, timing and memory manifest. For sequence models, save the complete training state needed for reconstruction rather than only aggregate metrics.

**Timing:** first run one corrected fold per retained architecture, preserving actual hyperparameters and data limits. C46's historical bundles record CPU execution with 15 epochs and up to 150,000 fit rows. Their old runtime does not establish the new budget. Extrapolate from measured fit/scoring time and number of required folds only after the clean-membership reuse audit.
